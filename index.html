About

I am interested in consciousness. I believe studying perception is a uniquely effective way to approach consciousness in the framework of neuroscience. Even though we cannot empirically observe the contents of perception, we can perform well-defined perceptual manipulations and rigorously measure perceptual outcomes.

My work combines EEG, fMRI, psychophysics, and computational analysis, plus causal manipulations with psychoactive compounds.

Active research areas

  • Psychedelic perception

    Mapping altered visual experience by combining neuroimaging and behavior with large-scale language analysis of first-person reports.

  • Perceptual multistability

    How the visual system selects one interpretation of an ambiguous image, and what holds it in place.

  • Attention and neural dynamics

    EEG studies of how attended and ignored object representations compete and evolve over time.
